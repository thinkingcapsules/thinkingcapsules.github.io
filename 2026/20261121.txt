In *With the Old Breed*, E. B. Sledge, a Marine at Peleliu and Okinawa, shows how men break: the shelling falls on someone already exhausted, sick, sleepless. [A reviewer](https://newsletter.osv.llc/p/osv-field-notes-45) notes that Sledge never calls them weak. After reading it, neither do you.

From outside you only see the last straw. Scripture looks at the whole load. After his victory on Mount Carmel, Elijah sits under a broom tree and asks to die: ["I have had enough, Lord"](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/1-kings_19-4-13.txt#v4). God doesn't answer with a lesson. An angel says ["get up and eat!"](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/1-kings_19-4-13.txt#v5), lets him sleep, feeds him again, ["or the journey ahead will be too much for you"](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/1-kings_19-4-13.txt#v7). The conversation comes later.

!!A breakdown is judged by the load, not by the last moment.!!
