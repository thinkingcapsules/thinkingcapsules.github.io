Zoos replaced bars with moats, notes [Leonardo Arigone](https://open.substack.com/pub/iltuffatore/p/tuffi177), an Italian philosopher, and it was better for the animals. But you can fight bars. Who rebels against a fence you can't see? Teachers no longer give summer homework; they wish you a nice summer. An order can be broken. A suggestion gives you nothing to push against.

He's right about soft control. He's wrong that freedom proves itself by breaking things. A plain command is a gift for the opposite reason: it can be refused, so obeying it means something. [Choose today whom you will serve](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/joshua_24-15.txt#v15). And Paul turns freedom away from the fence altogether: [use your freedom to serve one another in love](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/galatians_5-1-26.txt#v13).

!!A rule you can see is one you can refuse, and so one you can truly obey.!!
