[Henry Shevlin](https://open.substack.com/pub/bigthinkmedia/p/if-ai-becomes-conscious-how-would), a philosopher who works on animal and machine minds, asks how we would treat an AI if it turned out to be conscious. His answer comes from how we already treat animals. About 90% of adults believe animals feel pain consciously; only around a third would consider not eating them. And the influence runs backward: once eating them is in our interest, we start rating their minds lower. Evidence about machine minds keeps growing, he says, but evidence has rarely been what changes behavior. Convenience decides.

The diagnosis is accurate, and it isn't really about AI. It's how belief works whenever a fact would cost us something: [an unpleasant idea gets judged untrue first, and the reasons arrive later](post.html?p=2026/20261027.txt). Jeremiah said it earlier and harder: [the human heart is the most deceitful of all things](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/jeremiah_17-7-10.txt#v9).

Shevlin stops at the description, though. If interest bends what we're willing to see, more data won't fix it; the fix has to reach the interest. Proverbs doesn't wait for a theory of animal consciousness: [the godly care for their animals, but the wicked are always cruel](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/proverbs_12-7-18.txt#v10). Care comes first because it says something about who you are, before anything is settled about what they are. And the verse after Jeremiah's names the one judge convenience can't bribe: [I, the Lord, search all hearts and examine secret motives](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/jeremiah_17-7-10.txt#v10).

!!When a fact would cost you something, you start doubting the fact.!!
