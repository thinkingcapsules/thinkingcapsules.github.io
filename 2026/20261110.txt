2026_11_10 | More than any number

[Michael Huemer](https://open.substack.com/pub/fakenous/p/what-is-infinity), a philosopher who writes on ethics and the theory of knowledge, argues that infinity isn't a number. It's an indefinite idea: to call something infinite is to say that for every natural number n, there are more than n of it. The word works like "a great many" pushed all the way, not like a point further along the number line. Treat it as a quantity, as set theory has since Cantor, and you get rules no number obeys, like ∞ + 1 = ∞.

Whether mathematicians should follow him is their argument. Outside mathematics the point holds: "infinite" doesn't name the biggest amount. It names what no amount reaches.

That is how theology has always used the word for God. Anselm's definition, [that than which nothing greater can be conceived](post.html?p=2026/20260915.txt), isn't the top of a scale; it's what every scale falls short of. Isaiah gives the distance as an image: [just as the heavens are higher than the earth, so My ways are higher than your ways](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/isaiah_55-1-9.txt#v9). It has a practical edge. If God were only very large, enough effort, knowledge or virtue would close the gap in the end. Huemer's arithmetic shows why it can't: add as much as you like and you are still counting. The gap is crossed from the other side, by a God who comes down: [the Word became human and made His home among us](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/john_1-14.txt).

!!You don't reach the infinite by adding. It has to come to you.!!
