[Anne-Laure Le Cunff](https://bigthinkmedia.substack.com/p/why-smart-people-stay-stuck-in-research), a neuroscientist who writes about curiosity and learning, argues that too much research can be avoidance. Three things keep smart people collecting data: the urge to find the best possible option, the need for certainty when there's no rule for deciding, and the pleasure of thinking for its own sake. Reading never exposes you to rejection, uncertainty or embarrassment, and putting off the decision lifts your mood in the short term. Research helps until it reaches questions only experience can answer. Her remedy: list what you already know, run a small experiment with a deadline ("I'll do X for Y weeks"), note what surprised you, then decide whether to test again or go back to a narrower question.

She's right, and the trap is an old one: [most preparation is postponement that keeps its dignity](post.html?p=2026/20260427.txt). The rule for deciding that she finds missing does exist for some choices. Brian Christian and Tom Griffiths, in *[Algorithms to Live By](https://anthology-lab.github.io/books/algorithms-to-live-by-christian-griffiths.html)*, describe the 37% rule from the mathematics of optimal stopping: spend the first 37% of your time, or of the options, only looking, then take the first one that beats everything you've seen. Research gets a budget, and stopping becomes part of the method instead of a failure of it.

The experiment is a good tool, but it treats fear as a scheduling problem. The servant in Jesus' parable had Le Cunff's reasons. Given money to invest, he buried it and explained: [I was afraid I would lose your money, so I hid it in the earth](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/matthew_25-14-30.txt#v25). Underneath the caution was a picture of his master as harsh, and that picture made doing nothing look safe. Fear of failing usually rests on the same kind of belief: that a failure would say something final about you. Once your worth is settled somewhere else, the experiment is only an experiment. James is blunt about the alternative: [don't just listen to God's word, you must do what it says. Otherwise, you are only fooling yourselves](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/james_1-22.txt).

!!Research can't fail. That's what makes it a good place to hide.!!
