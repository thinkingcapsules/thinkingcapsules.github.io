"Action produces the very information inaction waits for," writes [Shane Parrish](https://fs.blog/brain-food/november-1-2026/). And before you start, being smart mostly makes your excuses more convincing.

That's the trap of careful people. Waiting feels like prudence, but what you're waiting to learn only shows up once you begin. [Research is a good place to hide](post.html?p=2026/20261113.txt). James is harsher: [don't just listen to God's word. You must do what it says. Otherwise, you are only fooling yourselves](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/james_1-22.txt#v22). Knowing without doing isn't a pause. It's a lie you tell yourself.

Parrish adds that spite makes you work harder. It does, and it keeps your eyes on the person you want to prove wrong. Faithfulness is better fuel: [it grows](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/galatians_5-1-26.txt#v22) from what you belong to, not from what you're against.

!!What you're waiting to know is on the other side of starting.!!
