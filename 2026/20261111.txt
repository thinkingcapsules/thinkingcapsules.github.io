![Castello Utveggio above Palermo. Photo: Radek Kucharski, CC BY 2.0](assets/notes/20261111-utveggio.jpg https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Castello_Utveggio_above_Palermo_%2852102250643%29.jpg)

[Vittorio Ray](https://open.substack.com/pub/iltuffatore/p/tuffi178), who writes the Italian newsletter Il Tuffatore, looks up from Palermo at Castello Utveggio on the slopes of Monte Pellegrino: a light that stays on while the city below falls apart, a beauty that refuses to be used up. He recalls the line attributed to Gustav Mahler: tradition is not the worship of ashes but the keeping of the fire.

The line is right, and the choice it describes is harder than it sounds. Ash is easier. It stays where you put it, asks nothing of you, and a cult can be built around it. [Fidelity that only preserves can end up arresting what it loves](post.html?p=2026/20260415.txt). A fire has to be fed every day or it goes out.

Where the image falls short is the place it gives you: below, looking up at a light that belongs to someone else. The Gospel puts the lamp in your hands: [you are the light of the world, like a city on a hilltop that cannot be hidden](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/matthew_5-1-7-29.txt), and no one lights a lamp to put it under a basket. Faith is kept the same way, by being lived, not admired. And the keeping doesn't rest on the keeper alone: [the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness can never extinguish it](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/john_1-1-5.txt#v5).

!!Ash stays where you put it. Fire has to be fed.!!
