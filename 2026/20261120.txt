When his father died, [Gregory Sadler](https://open.substack.com/pub/gregorybsadler/p/speculative-fiction-that-drew-me-e09) was twelve, and nobody at home knew how to talk about it. He found the words in fantasy novels: lonely, melancholy books whose heroes faced unfair losses and learned to trust again. Adults lent him the books they hoped would help.

That's love with the means at hand. Stories give grief words when no one can. The Psalms do the same: other people's grief, written so you can pray it as yours. [O Lord, how long will You forget me?](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/psalms_13-1-6.txt#v1) The difference is the address. A novel lets grief be felt. A lament sends it to Someone, and Psalm 13 ends in [trust](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/psalms_13-1-6.txt#v5), not because the loss is gone, but because the complaint went somewhere.

!!A story gives grief words. A lament gives it somewhere to go.!!
