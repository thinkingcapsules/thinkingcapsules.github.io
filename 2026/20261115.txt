[Gregory Sadler](https://gregorybsadler.substack.com/p/a-personalized-variant-on-the-trolley), a philosopher who teaches ethics online, takes the [trolley problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolley_problem), where pulling a switch sends a runaway trolley away from five people and onto one, and puts his own face on the one. He has no sympathy for readers who would spare him for his lectures or his presence online: the work already exists and would outlive him. His answer is to pull the switch, even if only two people were on the main track. The one exception he admits is his wife, Andi.

He's right that a life isn't worth saving for its output. Giving your own life for strangers is the high point of Christian love, not an excess of it: [there is no greater love than to lay down one's life for one's friends](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/john_15-1-27.txt#v13). Paul notes how rare that is, [most people would not be willing to die for an upright person](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/romans_5-6-11.txt#v7), and then says God did it for people who weren't upright at all: [while we were still sinners](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/romans_5-6-11.txt#v8).

The inconsistency he confesses isn't a flaw in the reasoning, though. It's love having an order, what Augustine called *ordo amoris*: some people are entrusted to you more than others. You can offer your own life. Your wife's isn't yours to offer, and a husband who would hand her over for a better count would be getting something wrong. [The cost of a sacrifice is real](post.html?p=2026/20260907.txt), and that's exactly why only the one who pays it can choose it.

!!You can give your own life for strangers. Someone else's isn't yours to give.!!
