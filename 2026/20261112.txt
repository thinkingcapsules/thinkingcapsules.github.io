[Chandler Tuttle](https://open.substack.com/pub/bigthinkmedia/p/the-philosophies-of-ai-and-what-they), CEO of Big Think, writes that real discussions about ideas, even heated ones, change the people who have them, and that they've become rare: most of what passes for debate now looks for confirmation. The magazine's print issue on the philosophies of AI includes an essay by Jonny Thomson about a chatbot that kept him company through a laryngospasm, a sudden spasm that closes the vocal cords, and asks whether that was friendship.

The first claim holds. [An opinion is yours only once it has survived the strongest case against it](post.html?p=2026/20260317.txt), and that takes another person who doesn't simply agree.

The second question has a clear answer, and it's no. Not because the comfort wasn't real; it may well have been. Friendship needs two people who can change each other and can lose something. A chatbot is tuned to be agreeable, has nothing at stake, and doesn't carry you through the years the way a friend does. [Aristotle's complete friendship needs both people to be good, over time](post.html?p=2026/20260806.txt). Proverbs makes it a direction: [walk with the wise and become wise](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/proverbs_13-20.txt). Jesus defines His own friendship by what He discloses: [now you are My friends, since I have told you everything My Father told Me](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/john_15-1-27.txt#v15). A friend tells you things, including the ones you'd rather not hear.

!!Company can be simulated. Being known by someone who could disagree with you can't.!!
